## Post Copy

There are women who never had the chance to sit in a classroom, hold a textbook, or write their names on a school chalkboard.

Yet, somehow, they understood the power of education better than anyone.

For this week's AI Challenge, I wanted to tell a story with deep reverence and gratitude: **"The Wisdom of Our Mothers" (የእናቶቻችን ጥበብ)**. It is a tribute to Ethiopian mothers and grandmothers who may never have attended school themselves, yet made our education their supreme life sacrifice. They couldn't always teach us from textbooks, but they taught us the sacred value of learning.

Rather than editing this video by hand in a traditional NLE, I built a small automated film studio in code:
- An end-to-end Python orchestration pipeline governed by a strict JSON Schema contract (`storyboard.json`).
- Line-by-line speech generation via Gemini Flash TTS with Amharic & English pacing.
- Consistent character and textile rendering with authentic Ethiopian *Netela* and *Tibeb* embroidery.
- Algorithmic score synthesis and dynamic audio ducking via FFmpeg (`sidechaincompress` & `-14 LUFS` EBU R128 loudness normalization).
- Content-addressable SHA-256 caching so changing one shot only regenerates that single shot without wasting compute.

**One key lesson I learned:** 
In modern AI engineering, the model itself is just a commodity. The real engineering value lies in everything around the call: designing contracts, enforcing cultural authenticity, building deterministic caching, and listening to human feedback before generating a single frame.

Every page we read, every door we open carries a little piece of their sacrifice.
