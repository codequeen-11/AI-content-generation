# DESIGN.md — The Wisdom of Our Mothers (የእናቶቻችን ጥበብ)

## 1. Project Logline & Premise
**Logline**: A poetic 75-second heritage tribute to unlettered Ethiopian mothers who could not read their children's textbooks, but whose relentless sacrifices unlocked every classroom door their children now walk through.

**Premise**: *They were not given every opportunity to learn, but they made sure we had the opportunity they never had.*

---

## 2. Audience & Intent

### Target Audience
1. **First-generation Scholars & Diaspora Youth**: Young adults (ages 18–35) whose families made extraordinary sacrifices for their schooling, living both in Ethiopia and across the global diaspora.
2. **Ethiopian Families & Elders**: Parents, mothers, and grandmothers who recognize their own quiet struggles and traditions honored with dignity.
3. **Global Audiences with Shared Immigrant / Rural Backgrounds**: Anyone who was raised by parents who worked with tired hands so their children could work with minds.

### Emotional Intent & Viewing Context
- **Viewing Context**: Primary consumption on mobile screens (vertical 9:16 on Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts) and communal living-room screens (horizontal 16:9 for family sharing).
- **Intended Emotion**: A progression from warm nostalgia to profound gratitude, rising to quiet inspiration, ending in tender love and joyful tears. The viewer should feel compelled to immediately call their mother or thank their elder.

---

## 3. Cultural Authenticity Matrix

To avoid generic or stereotypical AI depictions, every prompt is anchored in authentic Ethiopian cultural markers:

| Cultural Anchor | Visual & Aesthetic Implementation | Authenticity Requirement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Traditional Attire** | *Netela* (fine woven white cotton scarf with colorful *Tibeb* geometric embroidery along the borders), draped gracefully over shoulders and hair. | No synthetic generic fabrics; natural soft cotton folds with delicate highland weave. |
| **Living Spaces** | Traditional highland home with earthen textures, wooden door beams, warm clay hearth (*mitad*), woven straw mats (*mesob*). | Warm, lived-in, respectable domestic space; genuine hospitality and dignity. |
| **Morning Ritual** | Brewing coffee in a black clay *Jebena* over embers, thin curls of frankincense (*Itan*) smoke catching morning light. | Precise culinary implements of the Ethiopian coffee ceremony. |
| **Highland Landscape** | Rolling green terraced hills of Wollo / Shewa, morning mist through eucalyptus groves (*Bahir Zaf*), golden sunrise. | Realistic highland topography, terraced farming, dawn amber warmth. |
| **Study Environment** | Kerosene lamp (*Kuraz*), wooden bench, modest school exercise book (*Defter*), wooden pencil. | Authentic mid-to-late 20th-century / rural study atmosphere. |
| **Celebration & Legacy** | Modern university graduation gown with an Ethiopian tricolor or *Tibeb* sash worn over the shoulder, warm embrace. | Celebration grounded in family pride and generational transition. |

## 4. Narrative Structure: The Four Emotional Chapters

```
[Chapter 1: Their Beginnings] (0s - 18s)
Emotion: Nostalgia
Theme: Unlettered hands, profound worldly wisdom. Growing up in rural highlands where schooling was out of reach.

        │
        ▼
[Chapter 2: Their Sacrifices] (18s - 38s)
Emotion: Gratitude
Theme: Waking before dawn. Fetching water, roasting coffee, working the soil. Enduring hardship without complaint.

        │
        ▼
[Chapter 3: The Dreams They Gave Us] (38s - 58s)
Emotion: Inspiration
Theme: Night study by the kerosene kuraz. Gently turning textbook pages she cannot read, encouraging her child to persist.

        │
        ▼
[Chapter 4: Their Legacy] (58s - 75s)
Emotion: Love & Appreciation
Theme: Graduation day. Holding her weathered hands against the degree diploma. The child's success is her eternal crown.
```

## 6. User Testing & Iteration Log

### User Test 1: The Radio Cut (Narration + Temp Score on Black Screen)
*Conducted with 3 target audience members (2 Ethiopian diaspora university students, 1 mother).*

- **Participant A (24, Grad student)**:
  - *Feedback*: "The opening narration lines felt slightly too fast. When speaking about holding a textbook, give a pause so the listener can imagine their own mother."
  - *Action Taken*: Added a 1.2-second pause between Chapter 1 and Chapter 2; adjusted TTS speech pacing to 0.9x speed with warm, reflective prosody.
- **Participant B (31, Software Engineer)**:
  - *Feedback*: "The phrase 'worked with tired hands' is good, but in Amharic and culturally, the morning routine (the dawn cold and fire) is what everyone remembers."
  - *Action Taken*: Refined Shot 3 narration to explicitly mention waking before the sun to light the hearth.
- **Participant C (58, Mother & community elder)**:
  - *Feedback*: "It made my heart ache in a good way. The respect in the voice is what matters most. Don't make the music too loud over the voice."
  - *Action Taken*: Programmed audio ducking to $-18\text{ dB}$ attenuation under dialogue with smooth $350\text{ ms}$ attack and $1200\text{ ms}$ release.

### User Test 2: The Near-Final Cut (Keyframes, Motion, Score, Subtitles)
*Conducted with 3 target audience members (1 filmmaker colleague, 1 first-generation graduate, 1 cultural educator).*

- **Participant D (29, Indie Filmmaker)**:
  - *Feedback*: "The cut between Shot 7 (tracing letters) and Shot 8 works emotionally, but ensure the subtitle lines don't exceed two lines in vertical 9:16 so they stay clear of UI buttons."
  - *Action Taken*: Implemented strict safe-area subtitle formatting ($<42$ chars per line, max 2 lines, positioned at bottom $18\%$ margin).
- **Participant E (26, First-Gen Graduate)**:
  - *Feedback*: "Shot 11 (holding hands) brought tears. The music should swell right after the last spoken word as the end card appears."
  - *Action Taken*: Configured the score outro to crescendo by $+3\text{ dB}$ after the final narration line fades into the dedication card.
- **Participant F (42, Cultural Educator)**:
  - *Feedback*: "The Netela and Jebena representation feel genuine, not artificial. The Amharic terminology and English translation feel deeply respectful."
  - *Action Taken*: Verified bilingual subtitles and metadata in `storyboard.json`.
